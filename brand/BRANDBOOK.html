01 — The idea

A quotation mark that isn’t yours, around a dash that proves a human was here.

Why guillemets

Most English-speaking developers reach for " and stop thinking. The world does not: German nests „…“, French demands « … » with a narrow no-break space inside, Finnish closes with the same glyph it opens with. The guillemet is the most recognisable not-English quotation mark there is. Putting it in the logo states the thesis in one glyph: your language has its own rules, and this library knows them.

Why the em dash

In 2026 the em dash acquired a second, unearned meaning: circumstantial evidence that a machine wrote the text. That is a typographic tragedy — the em dash is simply the correct character, and it was correct long before anyone trained a language model. We put it at the centre of the mark, framed and unmissable. An em dash is not a confession. It is punctuation, set properly.

What the mark says without a caption

Reading Meaning
Guillemets = multilingual Ten locales cited, five runtimes designed for. Not an English-only tool.
Em dash = correctness The right character, in the right place, at the right width.
Enclosure = a transform Text goes in, corrected text comes out. transform(input, options).
Symmetry = idempotency The mark is its own mirror. So is the engine: transform(transform(x)) === transform(x).

The mark contains no letters, no language and no country. That is deliberate: polytypo is a spec with interchangeable implementations, and the logo has to survive a Go port, a PHP port and a CMS plugin without ever looking like it belongs to one of them.

02 — Construction

Drawn on a 200 × 120 grid.

Every asset in this kit derives from one geometry. Reproduce the mark from these numbers, never by tracing an exported PNG or by typing « — » in a text field.

Construction drawing. Grid unit = 10. Chevron arms run 14 across and 18 up, giving an apex of about 75.6°. Stroke 9 with round caps and joins. The em dash is a rectangle, 84 × 9, with flat terminals — because that is how an em dash is cut in type, while the guillemet in most text faces is a stroke with a soft end.

Element Geometry (grid units)
Canvas 200 × 120, mark centred on y = 60
Chevron path (outer left) M28 42 L14 60 L28 78
Chevron path (inner left) M44 42 L30 60 L44 78
Chevron pitch 16 units between apexes
Stroke 9, round cap, round join
Em dash rect x 58, y 55.5, w 84, h 9
Dash-to-chevron gap 9.5 optical (14 nominal, less the round cap)
Ink bounding box 181 × 45 — the standalone mark’s viewBox

Why the dash is that long

In type, an em dash is one em wide — the single most-recognisable length in punctuation. A short bar reads as a hyphen, and a hyphen is exactly the error this library exists to fix. The dash is therefore 84 units against a 45-unit mark height: nearly twice as long as the mark is tall, and visibly longer than the two guillemets combined. If a rendering makes you hesitate about whether it is an em dash or a hyphen, the rendering is wrong.

03 — The logo system

One mark, five sanctioned lockups.

Pick by container shape, not by taste. If none of the five fits, the answer is a different container, not a sixth lockup.

Inverse and single-colour

The mark is monochrome by definition. It has no gradient, no two-tone version and no “dark mode variant” beyond swapping ink for paper. In HTML, prefer polytypo-mark-current.svg or polytypo-lockup-current.svg — they paint with currentColor and follow the surrounding theme automatically.

04 — Clear space & minimum sizes

The mark needs a margin as wide as its dash is thick — twice over.

Minimum sizes

Asset Screen Print
Horizontal lockup 140 px wide 30 mm wide
Stacked lockup 96 px wide 22 mm wide
Mark alone 44 px wide 10 mm wide
Favicon tile 32 px (16 px fallback) —
Avatar 48 px —

Tile padding

Square tiles — avatar, favicon, sticker, app icon — are padded in the same unit as clear space: 2X on each side, giving a mark that occupies 83.4% of the tile’s width. The favicon tile is the single exception at 1X (90.9% of width), because below 48 px the clear space costs more than it protects. There is no third value: if a tile needs different padding, it is the wrong tile.

Below the mark’s minimum, switch to the favicon tile or to the wordmark. Do not thicken the strokes to compensate, and never redraw the mark with fewer chevrons or a shorter dash to make it fit — that is a different logo, and it is the exact error this one exists to argue against.

How they are used

  • Ink — the mark, headlines, body text. Not pure black: pure black on a screen reads as a hole.
  • Paper — backgrounds, inverse mark. Warm, not white; the brand should feel printed.
  • Correction Red — the proof-reader’s pencil. One accent per view, maximum: a link, a diff marker, a corrected character in a demo, a limited merch run. Never the mark in a product UI.
  • Graphite and its tints — captions, rules, disabled states, code chrome.

CMYK and Pantone equivalents above are conversions, not measured proofs. Confirm against the printer’s own profile before any production run.

Contrast

Ink on Paper is 15.9:1. Paper on Correction Red is 4.0:1 — acceptable for the logo and for text at 19 px and above, not for small body copy. Never set Correction Red on Ink.

06 — Typography

Inter for language. JetBrains Mono for machines.

Inter — wordmark, headlines, body

SIL Open Font Licence 1.1 · variable · Latin, Cyrillic, Greek

Chosen for coverage, not for fashion: a project that ships German, Russian, Finnish, Swedish and French examples needs one face that renders all of them without a fallback stack. Its flat terminals and even rhythm also match the mark’s straight-cut dash.

« Kurz » — „Zitat“ — ”lainaus”

Inter SemiBold 600 · the wordmark is set at weight 600 with −0.8% tracking, then outlined, so no asset depends on the font being installed.

JetBrains Mono — code, rule ids, code points

SIL Open Font Licence 1.1 · variable

Used wherever the text is machine-readable: API snippets, rule ids, error codes, code points and captions in this document. It distinguishes hyphen, en dash and em dash at small sizes, which matters more here than anywhere else. 

transform("Il a dit: «bonjour»!", { locale: "fr" })
// → "Il a dit : « bonjour » !"   U+202F, U+00AB, U+00BB

07 — Writing the brand

The one brand that has no excuse for a straight quote.

The name

  • Always polytypo, always lowercase — including at the start of a sentence.
  • Never PolyTypo, Polytypo, POLYTYPO or poly-typo.
  • The mark may be written inline as «—» in running text, e.g. a CLI banner.
  • It is “a microtypography engine”, not “a typography library for JavaScript” — the spec is the product, the runtimes are replaceable.

House typography rules

  • All public copy is run through polytypo itself before it ships. If our own README fails the conformance suite, we have shipped a lie.
  • Straight quotes and straight apostrophes never appear in prose. Only in code samples, where they are the input being fixed.
  • Rule ids, locale codes and code points always in mono: quotes, de-CH, U+202F.
  • Code points are written as U+00A0, never as an invisible literal character in prose.

Voice

Flat, specific, checkable. Every claim in polytypo’s documentation is backed by a citation, a fixture or a test — the brand voice inherits that. No superlatives, no “beautiful typography”, no promises about craft. State what the rule is, cite who says so, show the before and after.

08 — Misuse

Eight ways to break it.

The worst one is the last: setting the “logo” by typing « — » in a text field. The result inherits whatever face is installed, kerns differently on every platform, and turns the em dash into whatever the fallback font thinks a dash is. The logo is a drawing. Use the file.

10 — Merch programme

A mark this good deserves to leave the screen.

polytypo makes no money, by design — the project brief rules out monetisation entirely. Merch follows the same rule: priced at cost, produced in small runs, given away at conferences and to contributors. It exists to put an argument on a person’s chest, not to open a revenue line.

What goes where

The mark survives almost any surface, because it is one colour, has no fine detail and no enclosed counters. What changes from surface to surface is only the weight of the stroke and the minimum size. Two artworks cover everything: the regular mark, and polytypo-mark-heavy.svg — the same geometry with a 22% thicker stroke, for anything textured, stitched, engraved or very small.

Rules that hold on every surface

  • One colour. No second ink inside the mark, no outline, no shadow, no distressed texture.
  • Horizontal, always. The mark is never rotated to follow a seam, a sleeve or a mug’s curve.
  • Clear space 2X, and seams, hems, zips and pockets count as intruders.
  • Where the surface is textured, stitched, engraved or below 40 mm, switch to the heavy cut rather than thickening the regular one by hand.
  • Never redraw the mark to fit a shape — fewer chevrons or a shorter dash is a different logo.

Slogans

  • “The em dash was mine.” slogan-em-dash-was-mine.svg — the hero. A joke to everyone and a manifesto to writers. Front of tee and hoodie, under the mark.
  • “U+2014” slogan-u2014.svg — the insider cut. Back neck, cap, sticker, pin card. Says the same thing to people who already know.
  • “Locale-correct by default” slogan-locale-correct.svg — the conference cut: explains the project to a stranger from across a hallway.

Slogans are set in Inter Medium and always sit below the mark, centred to it, with a gap of at least 3X. Never beside it, never inside the clear space, never in place of the wordmark on primary applications.

The stroke-width rule

The mark’s stroke is 4.97% of its printed width. So stroke (mm) = 0.0497 × print width (mm). Embroidery needs at least 1.4 mm of stroke, which puts a hard floor on the regular mark at 28 mm wide; below that, or on any textured or knitted surface, use polytypo-mark-heavy.svg, whose stroke is 22% thicker. Screen print floor is 1.2 mm (24 mm wide); DTG, 1.5 mm (30 mm wide).

Regenerating

python3 brand/tools/gen_svg.py     # vectors, wordmark outlined from Inter
node   brand/tools/render.mjs      # PNG exports and the social card

The wordmark is outlined at build time, so no delivered asset requires Inter to be installed. Fonts are downloaded on demand and are not vendored into the repository.
